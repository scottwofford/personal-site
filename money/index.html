Money

Anti-consumerism as a value, optimization as a game. The goal is financial independence, not stuff.

The Philosophy

Two Lil Dicky songs cover most of it:

  • $ave Dat Money - he shot an entire music video with mansions, yachts, and a Lamborghini by asking to borrow everything for free. Spending less isn't deprivation, it's a game you can win.
  • Earth - the flip side. Consumerism isn't just expensive for you; the planet pays for it too.

The question I try to ask before spending: even if we can afford it, do we need it? Every recurring expense I don't have is one less thing financial independence has to pay for. A lower burn rate is a double win: you save more now, and the finish line moves closer.

The Mechanisms

Nothing here is exotic. The edge is treating each one as a game with rules you actually read.

Credit card points

  • Sign-up bonuses are most of the value. Track the minimum-spend requirement and its deadline like a project: amount, date opened, date the bonus posts.
  • Track the annual-fee date too. Know the date you have to cancel or downgrade before the fee hits, and put it on the calendar when you open the card.
  • Transfer partners beat cash redemption. Points transferred to airline programs are usually worth more than the flat cash-back rate. Know your card's partners and ratios.
  • Set a floor. Only redeem when the value per point clears a minimum (cents per point); otherwise hold or pick a different redemption.
  • Simple default: one flat-rate travel card for general purchases, category cards only where they clearly beat it.

Cash and investing

  • Cash sits in high-yield savings, not a checking account earning nothing.
  • Asset allocation is the primary driver of long-term returns. Get that right first; individual picks matter far less.
  • Passive, low-fee index funds. Fees compound against you the same way returns compound for you.
  • Max the tax-advantaged accounts before investing in taxable: 401(k), backdoor Roth IRA, 529s for kids.
