Philosophy Ranking AI Outcomes Wrong on Internet

Ranking AI Outcomes by Benefit to Humanity

Source: MATS Autumn 2026 application (application, Notion version). WIP and subject to change.

My North Star

In priority order:

  1. Don't go extinct as a species (the ultimate one way door).
  2. Don't descend into chaos / anarchy / authoritarianism (don't reduce quality of life significantly).
  3. Increase human well-being: health, wealth, agency/autonomy (not sure about order since it depends on starting point).

My test for each option: 1) my daughter's life outlook, 2) Luciana (a low/mid-income Colombian child) or a street kid in India's life outlook.

The Ranking

1. Building defenses against AI-enabled biological threats. Don't go extinct as a species via an AI-accelerated pandemic (not at all hard to imagine given humanity's incompetence dealing with COVID). In Toby Ord's Precipice, engineered pandemics are roughly 1 in 30 this century, second only to unaligned AI.

2. Building oversight mechanisms that detect when AI systems pursue unintended goals. I have personally spent 30+ hours per week with Claude or ChatGPT or Gemini since May 2024, outside of my job at Amazon. Based on my lived experience with these LLMs and their harnesses like Claude Code, it seems to me AI systems today often pursue unintended goals, i.e. the types of prosaic misalignment that Luthien was designed to solve. And therefore it seems very easy to imagine that with the scaling laws and the economic incentives to deploy AI in the economy, it will become increasingly common for AI systems to unintentionally pursue goals that the humans that instantiate them didn't intend. And so if I look forward five or even 50 years, the risk of this without robust real time oversight mechanisms is very high and likely. And the strength of our current oversight mechanisms relative to the speed at which AI capabilities are advancing are humblingly terrible. Ord puts unaligned AI at about 1 in 10, his highest single existential estimate.

3. Detecting and labeling AI-generated misinformation and synthetic media. The scenarios I'm most worried about are where AI amplifies misinformation or sycophancy or generally creates chaos due to bad design or just prosaic misalignment. Taking COVID as an example, misinformation was already a huge deal. And so, in an AI-accelerated pandemic in which AI contributes to more misinformation and fake news, it seems very likely that the death toll of the new pandemic would be an order of magnitude higher at least compared to COVID.

4. Minimizing unrestricted access to state-of-the-art AI chips. It's good to have the option to pause, because the option takes time to build and we could find ourselves in a scenario that leads to catastrophic loss of control downside, and we'd be sad if we didn't have the option. However, using the option has a very high cost due to second order effects and my libertarian bias.

5. Accelerating AI applications in scientific research. I'm a huge proponent of accelerating AI in science. Getting closer to epistemic truth seems 99% good with less risk.

6. Building AI-powered robotic systems that can learn from physical interaction with their environment. Broadly useful, with the main downsides being military applications, and it seems like the cat's already out of the bag on that with drones, so the marginal contribution of this relative to the upside seems favorable.

7. Reducing the environmental impact of AI data centers. Climate change is important, but not existential. In Ord's framing, climate is about 1 in 1,000 this century versus unaligned AI at about 1 in 10, and he thinks climate risk has fallen since 2020. Frontier training power is doubling roughly every year; single runs are projected to draw 4 to 16 GW by 2030. What matters here isn't raw energy, it's emissions and local effects. Even at 10x, data centers stay a small single-digit share of global emissions; the bigger lever is regional grid stress, electricity-price spikes, and water use.

8. Ensuring fair compensation for workers displaced by AI automation. I'm more optimistic on economic paradigms where creativity and agency are the dominating source of purpose compared to UBI-like systems. I do think a basic safety net of helping people feel safe and meet basic needs is a great investment, given that about 8.5% of the population makes less than $2.15/day (about 10.3% under the newer $3/day line). The literature on well-being up to a certain point of income is robust, but I think it's hard to measure who was "displaced," so better to use needs-based economic redistribution vs. social justice-based economic redistribution.

9. Protecting creators' work from unauthorized use in AI training data. Only ~2% to ~6% of humans, and it's not clear how severely this negatively affects their well-being.

10. Reducing the computational cost of training frontier AI models. I think the market is taking care of this. Worth separating two things: cost per unit of capability is falling fast (algorithmic and hardware efficiency), while absolute frontier training cost is rising about 2.4x a year. So the market takes care of efficiency, not total spend or energy.
